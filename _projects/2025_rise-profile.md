Reaching for Inclusion, Striving for Excellence in Computing Stars (RISE Stars) is a targeted initative, intervention, and socio-academic counterspace aimed at broadening participation in the computing. We sought to do so by supporting the social, academic and professional success of freshmen and sophomore Black women in computer science at Georgia State University. I created and led this program for two years alongside Dr. Anu Bourgeois, a full professor in the Department of Computer Science. During my time I created several workshops and events for students to learn advanced programming concepts, take steps to break into the technical industry and bond with like-minded peers. A core component of the program was near-peer mentorship, where upperclass students mentored the Stars to create full stack technical projects and later computing research projects. Many of the students in this program secured internships at top companies like Uber, Duolingo, GoDaddy, and Microsoft. We published the results of this program at ACM SIGCSE 2025 and are actively working on additional publications. Please explore the projects that students created over the years below!

### Technical Projects

Since the beginning of the RISE stars program, we challenge students in the Fall semester to create a social impact project. Originally, the goal was to focus on the design of wireframes in Figma. But quickly, we noticed that students wished to make full stack projects instead. So I adapted and found strategies to teach freshmen and sophomore students the basics of frontend and backend development. Amongst these strategies was the support and dedication of upperclass students! Please take a look at that projects below:

  Authors: Vanhora Gaspard, Alaya McCall, Martha Mwangi, Chenice Taylor
  Description: Mentored by Ashlyn Campbell, students created a solution to combat human trafficking. PanthersProtect offers updated news, safety tips, and information about hotspots near Georgia State University. The website includes a hotspot map and testimony chat to raise awareness and empower users.

### Research Projects

In the second year of the program, I had the neat idea to introduce students to research in the Spring semester. This idea came from my growing interest in research as well as an understanding of how difficult it can be to break into research as an undergrad. Similar to the technical projects, students were paired with upperclass students to successfully outline their research design. The students seemed to throughly enjoy this opportunity and presented at the Georgia State Undergraduate Research Conference (GSURC). Please take a look at the projects below:
