In 2023, I was awarded the Benjamin Gilman International Scholarship to study abroad in **Bali, Indonesia** through Georgia State University’s _Bali Adventure: Culture & Sustainability_ program. As a Gilman Scholar, it is my responsibility to share resources with other students interested in studying abroad ([view my tips](#gilman-tips)).
Additionally, I have documented each day like a story: the food, the ceremonies, the homestays, the NGOs we visited, and the people who shaped the experience. This page is an extension of my follow-on service project or [study abroad profile](/profile/): a visual and narrative walkthrough of those 11 days in Bali.

        <h4 class="section-header">Day 6 – Rice fields and community based sustainability</h4>
        <p>
          Day six was muddy and extremely fun. We learned about the Subak system from with the Five Pillar organization, then walked through rice fields with mud up to our knees and cows nearby. It is not something I would have imagined myself doing before the trip, 
          but in Bali it felt like the right answer was why not? I realized it was no better time than with Five Pillar, which focuses on sustainable tourism that supports rural communities, 
          local culture and the environment. Later we visited Kopernik, a non governmental organization (NGO) that experiments with solutions for people living in the last mile. Through projects like Perfect Fit, The Magi Farm and Rumbia, they work on reusable period products, 
          renewable energy and food waste processing. Hearing about the stigma around periods and how reusable period underwear is helping people push back 
          against it was one of the biggest takeaways of the day.
        </p>

  <div class="container">
    <h4 class="section-header">Gilman Scholarship Application Tips</h4>

    <p>
      Applying for the Benjamin A. Gilman International Scholarship is less about sounding perfect
      and more about being honest and specific about who you are. Your background, identities,
      academic interests, and lived experiences are the core of your application—use them to explain
      why studying abroad matters to you and how it connects to your long-term goals.
    </p>

    <p>
      I found it helpful to treat each essay as a different angle on the same story: why this program,
      why now, and how you’ll bring the experience back to your communities. Below are the more detailed
      notes I used from my study abroad office while writing my own Gilman application. The best advice possible 
      is to get as many readers to look at your papers.
    </p>

    <details class="gilman-tips">
      <summary><i class="fa-solid fa-caret-down"></i> Show full tips</summary>

      <div class="tips-flow">

        <div class="tips-panel">
          <p><strong>Statement of Purpose</strong></p>
          <ul>
            <li>Introduce yourself beyond your major: include relevant parts of your identity, background, and experiences.</li>
            <li>Connect your academic path (major, minor, key classes, projects) directly to the program and location.</li>
            <li>Be specific about what you hope to gain: skills, perspectives, networks, or knowledge not available at home.</li>
            <li>End with how the program supports your future academic or career plans.</li>
          </ul>
        </div>

        <div class="tips-panel">
         <p><strong>Building Mutual Understanding</strong></p>
          <ul>
            <li>Explain how you will engage meaningfully with the host community (e.g., homestays, language practice, local groups, etc.).</li>
            <li>Highlight reciprocity: what you hope to learn and what perspectives you bring.</li>
            <li>Avoid tourist language, this is about learing so you should emphasize, curiosity, humility, and respect for local knowledge.</li>
          </ul>
        </div>

        <div class="tips-panel">
         <p><strong>Follow-On Service Project</strong></p>

          <ul>
            <li>Identify a specific audience who would benefit from your project.</li>
            <li>Outline a realistic plan: what the project is, when it will happen, and how you’ll deliver it.</li>
            <li>Explain how your project expands access to global learning and encourages more students to apply.</li>
          </ul>
        </div>

      </div>
    </details>
  </div>
